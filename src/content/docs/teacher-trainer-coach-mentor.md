The same person can teach, train, coach or mentor, and the modes are not interchangeable. Picking the right one decides whether a session adds knowledge or confidence, and platform learning needs both.

## Teacher vs Trainer vs Coach vs Mentor
A teacher delivers knowledge in a structured course, and a trainer builds a specific skill through repetition. A coach stays alongside someone during a task and corrects performance as it happens. A mentor shares lived experience and long-term guidance, often without a fixed schedule. The roles overlap, and good technical leaders switch between them constantly. ServiceNow onboarding needs all four: platform teaching, drill practice, live support, and career direction.

### 1. Teacher
Essence: Explains knowledge.
### Core goal
A teacher builds foundational understanding. Learners need to know what a thing is and why it behaves that way before touching a keyboard. In ServiceNow terms, a teacher explains what a Configuration Item is and why the CMDB exists at all. Without that frame, a newcomer memorizes clicks and cannot adapt when the form changes. Comprehension lands first; the tool follows second.
### Typical situation
Teaching runs on a schedule. A week might cover Incident Management, with a lab at the end and a quiz on Friday. Somebody owns the curriculum and the learning objectives. The format suits onboarding batches and certification prep, where everyone starts near the same place. The calendar drives everything, so a learner who falls behind waits for the next cohort.
### Relationship style
The trainer's relationship stays formal but flattens out. The trainer still leads the session, yet works beside the learner at the same keyboard. Authority rests on demonstrated competence rather than on a title or a syllabus. Questions flow both ways, because the trainer learns where the current configuration trips people up. Respect replaces rank as the binding force.
### Measure of success
A teacher succeeds when learners recall and explain material on demand. Test scores and quiz results give the clearest evidence, because somebody who cannot describe a table relationship has not learned it. Interview questions work as a second measure. The real signal stays verbal: the learner restates the concept in their own words and handles a follow-up question without notes.
### Example in our world
A teacher walks a class through the CMDB, covering what it is, what a Configuration Item is, and where CI Class Manager fits. The session ends with an explanation of class inheritance and identification rules rather than a configuration task. Learners leave able to describe how the CMDB models a server. The goal is the mental model, not a finished record.
### Risk or limitation
Teaching turns hollow when it never touches a real instance. Learners pass quizzes and then freeze at the first blank form. A class on ACLs that never shows a blocked request leaves people unable to diagnose one. Counter this by pairing every concept session with a hands-on exercise in a sub-production instance, or the theory evaporates within a week.

### 4. Mentor
A mentor invests in long-term growth and direction rather than in this week's deliverable. The mentor asks where somebody wants to be in three years, then shapes assignments to close that gap. Typical moves include handing over a design review, making an introduction at a user group, or steering a person away from a dead-end specialization. The relationship outlives any single project.

### Quick comparison
| Role | Focus | Time horizon | Typical style | Success looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teacher | Knowledge / concepts | Long-term foundation | Explains | I understand it |
| Trainer | Skill / execution | Short-term delivery | Demonstrates & drills | I can do it correctly |
| Coach | Performance / behavior / mindset | Near-term improvement | Challenges & refines | I can do it better, under pressure |
| Mentor | Career / identity / direction | Long-term journey | Guides & sponsors | I’m becoming who I want to become |
### Easiest way to remember
A teacher explains how routing works. A trainer builds the firewall rule while the learner watches the console. A coach asks what stalled last week's change and what the learner will do differently on Monday. A mentor hands over an architecture review, because design work points at the career that person wants. Four roles, four verbs: explain, show, question, invest.
### Clarity: common confusion in companies
A manager is not automatically a mentor, and a strong trainer is not automatically a coach. Companies collapse all four roles into one job title and then wonder why onboarding stalls. The manager owns your performance rating, which makes honest career talk harder. The trainer owns a skill transfer. Keep the roles separate on paper, even when one person wears two hats.
### Final distilled version
### The four roles in a ServiceNow Academy
A ServiceNow Academy needs all four roles working at once. A teacher covers platform concepts before anyone configures. A trainer runs labs on Incident Management, Flow Designer, and CMDB class models. A coach keeps each learner moving through a slow certification path. A mentor points the strongest people at architecture or platform ownership. Cut one role, and the program produces theorists or button-pushers.
